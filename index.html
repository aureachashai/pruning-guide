Hydrangeas

Grouped by the only question that matters at the shrub: can I cut this one hard? Same genus, opposite answers. Cutting an old-wood hydrangea hard costs you a season of flowers.

✂️

Blooms on NEW wood plants: 2

Safe to cut hard in early spring. It flowers on growth it makes this year.

Hydrangea arborescens ‘Annabelle’

pruneCut stems to 12–18" from ground in early spring; remove a few of the oldest canes each year to renew. Strong renewal pruning keeps flowers large.Early Spring

deadheadDeadhead in fall if desired.Mid Fall

Hydrangea paniculata

prunePrune for shape and size. Can be pruned back to old wood or even to the ground and still flower that same year (though blooms will be later). Thin weak or crossing stems annually.Early Spring

deadheadDeadhead to tidy but optional for winter interest.As Needed

🚫

Blooms on OLD wood plants: 3

Do NOT cut hard. Next year's flowers are already on the stems — prune lightly, after flowering.

Hydrangea macrophylla — Wedding Gown / Paris

pruneLight pruning in early spring — remove weak or dead stems. Do not cut hard; blooms form on old wood.Early Spring

pruneRemove about one third of the old stems, and cut back by roughly six inches — adjust to how vigorous the plant is and how tall it has grown.Early Spring

deadheadDeadhead after flowering to tidy but avoid heavy pruning.Late Summer

Hydrangea petiolaris

prunePrune immediately after flowering. Faded flowers can be trimmed to a node. Avoid pruning too hard; stems form flowers next year.Early Summer

Hydrangea quercifolia

prunePrune selectively in spring using "50/50 renewal" method. Remove oldest stems to a bud point to renew shrub. Prune carefully to prevent floppy growth.Early Spring

deadheadDeadhead flowers when finished, or leave until spring.After Flowering
